What it shows

  • Line of sight matters. Removing a metal rod raised the mean SNR by 12 dB (16.4 → 28.4 dB).
  • The real room is rugged. 261 local peaks in the measured map against 32 in the simulation.
  • So uphill-only methods fail. Greedy, random and pattern search pass 42–52% on the real map, against 84–90% on the simulated one.
  • Coarse-to-fine and Bayesian handle it: 96–100% pass.

What it shows

  • Bayesian is the best rotation search: fewest measurements, 98% within 2 dB.
  • Coarse-to-fine works on clean data (100%) but missed all 3 live runs: one high reading misleads it.
  • Lobing only finds the nearest peak: 52% from a random start.
  • Pointing vs moving: panning alone (antenna on the axis) changes SNR by ~4 dB; the whole map spans 19.5 dB, mostly from the antenna swinging as it tilts.
